# Results — BRFSS Mental Health Analysis

## Analysis 2: National Depression Trend (2022–2024)

| Year | Average Depression Rate (%) |
|------|----------------------------|
| 2022 | 21.3% |
| 2023 | 21.1% |
| 2024 | 21.8% |

**Finding:** National depression prevalence has remained essentially flat
over three years (2022–2024), showing no meaningful improvement. This
suggests population-level mental health interventions have not achieved
measurable impact during this period.

## Analysis 3: Loneliness vs Poor Mental Health Days

| Pattern | Observation |
|---------|-------------|
| Correlation direction | Positive — higher loneliness = more poor mental health days |
| Highest burden states | Louisiana, Georgia, Mississippi |
| Lowest burden states | South Dakota, North Carolina |
| Range of loneliness (% often/always lonely) | 26% – 36% across states |
| Range of poor mental health days | 6% – 20% across states |

**Finding:** A clear positive correlation exists between loneliness and
poor mental health days at the state level. States with the highest
loneliness rates consistently show the most poor mental health days,
identifying social isolation as a key modifiable risk factor.

---

## Analysis 4: Life Satisfaction vs Depression

| Pattern | Observation |
|---------|-------------|
| Correlation direction | Negative — higher satisfaction = lower depression |
| Highest depression + lowest satisfaction | West Virginia (~91.5% satisfied, 30.2% depressed) |
| Lowest depression + highest satisfaction | US Virgin Islands (~97% satisfied, ~9.5% depressed) |
| Life satisfaction range across states | 91% – 97% |
| Depression rate range | 13% – 30% |

**Finding:** Strong negative correlation between life satisfaction and
depression prevalence. States where residents report lower life satisfaction
consistently show higher depression rates, suggesting wellbeing-focused
public health programmes could reduce depression burden.

## Analysis 5: Multi-Indicator Heatmap Summary

Top 15 states by depression rate show consistently poor performance
across multiple mental health indicators:

| Indicator | Average in High-Burden States |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Depression diagnosis | 26–30% |
| Poor mental health days | 15–20% |
| Loneliness (often/always) | 32–36% |
| Social support (always/usually) | ~85% |
| Life satisfaction | 91–93% |

**Finding:** High-depression states show a cluster of poor outcomes
across all mental health indicators — depression does not occur in
isolation but alongside loneliness, poor mental health days, and
lower life satisfaction. This supports a holistic, multi-factor
approach to mental health intervention.

## Summary of Key Findings

| Finding | Value | Implication |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| National depression rate | ~21-22% | 1 in 5 adults affected |
| Trend 2022-2024 | Flat (no improvement) | Interventions not working at scale |
| Highest burden state | West Virginia (30.2%) | Targeted policy needed |
| Loneliness correlation | Positive and strong | Social isolation = modifiable risk factor |
| Life satisfaction correlation | Negative and strong | Wellbeing programmes may reduce depression |
| Geographic pattern | Southern/Appalachian states | Socioeconomic factors likely driving disparities |
